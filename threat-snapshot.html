    Key trends & notable developments

      Long-term trends

      Reports published per period, from the archive that grows every 3 hours. Counts are reports (publications), not de-duplicated incidents.

      Publicly disclosed financial impact

      Only amounts a source explicitly ties to a cyber incident are listed. Ransom demands, estimates and allegations are shown but never added to the headline total, and an amount repeated across articles on one incident counts once.

      Methodology & sources

      This dashboard summarizes publicly available reporting. It shows what trusted publishers and regulators have reported, not a complete or independently verified view of global attacks. A rise in reports can reflect more media coverage rather than more attacks.

      How numbers are counted

      • Reports analyzed: distinct articles, advisories and filings published in the window.
      • Incidents: reports are grouped when they share a CVE, or when headlines published within 5 days share at least three significant words (similarity ≥ 50%). One group is one incident.
      • Confirmed incidents: incidents confirmed by a first-party source (regulator filing, government advisory) or where the report cites the organization’s own confirmation. Threat-actor claims are labelled Unconfirmed claim.
      • APT activity: distinct incidents or campaigns that mention nation-state activity or a named APT group.
      • Ransomware activity: distinct incidents classified as ransomware.
      • Critical vulnerabilities: distinct vulnerability stories rated critical (CVSS 9+ or described as critical) or reported as actively exploited.
      • Disclosed financial impact: summed per currency (no conversion) for directly disclosed costs, fines, theft losses, business-interruption losses and ransom reported as paid, excluding alleged or estimated figures and ransom demands.
      • Severity is an automated estimate from the text (for example exploitation, ransomware or nation-state involvement) and is not an official rating.
      • Period-over-period change appears only after two full windows of data have been collected; otherwise it reads “Insufficient data”.
      • Geography uses the location stated in the report. A suspected attacker origin is shown only when the source states it and is never inferred from IP data.

      Classification is rule-based and can be wrong. Always read the original source before acting on an item.
